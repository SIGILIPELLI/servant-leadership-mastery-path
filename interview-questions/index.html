    Servant Leadership Interview Questions and Answers¶

    180 questions with the correct answer and a short explanation, organised from beginner (Level 1) to advanced (Level 4). Every question comes from a lesson in the free Servant Leadership Mastery Path, so you can open the lesson to go deeper.

    Practise, don't just read

    Each lesson also ends with an interactive quiz, and the final quiz tests all four levels. Try to answer each question below before reading the answer.

    Level 1 · Entry¶

    01 · What Is Servant Leadership?¶

    Q1. Who introduced the term 'servant leadership' in his 1970 essay?

    Answer: Robert K. Greenleaf

    Greenleaf's essay 'The Servant as Leader' began the modern movement.

    Q2. What question did Greenleaf suggest as a test of servant leadership?

    Answer: Do those served grow as persons, becoming healthier, wiser, freer and more autonomous, and do they become servants themselves?

    The test is about the growth of the people served.

    Q3. How does servant leadership differ most from traditional top-down leadership?

    Answer: It starts from serving people's growth and needs so they can do their best work, rather than from authority and control

    Servant leaders still set direction and hold standards.

    Q4. Which statement is a common misunderstanding of servant leadership?

    Answer: That it means being permissive or never making hard decisions

    It is not weakness: it pairs high care with high standards.

    02 · Self-Awareness & Emotional Intelligence Basics¶

    Q5. Which is one of the four domains in Goleman's emotional intelligence model?

    Answer: Self-awareness (alongside self-management, social awareness and relationship management)

    The four domains are self-awareness, self-management, social awareness and relationship management.

    Q6. You snap at a colleague whenever someone interrupts you. Which skill is the first step?

    Answer: Noticing and naming the trigger (self-awareness) before choosing a response

    You cannot manage a trigger you have not noticed.

    Q7. What is the difference between a trigger and a reaction?

    Answer: A trigger is the event or cue; the reaction is your chosen or automatic response to it

    The gap between them is where choice lives.

    Q8. Why is emotional self-awareness relevant to servant leaders?

    Answer: They cannot serve others well while unaware of how their own moods and biases shape their behaviour

    Awareness protects the team from your blind spots.

    03 · Active Listening¶

    Q9. Which is the deepest of the three levels of listening?

    Answer: Global (or contextual) listening, noticing tone, energy and the whole situation

    Levels: internal, focused and global.

    Q10. Your report says 'I'm fine, it's just a lot.' Which response is active listening?

    Answer: 'It sounds like the workload is heavy. What feels hardest right now?'

    Paraphrase and invite elaboration.

    Q11. What is paraphrasing for?

    Answer: Checking you understood and showing the speaker they were heard

    Reflect meaning, not just words.

    Q12. What does multitasking during a one-to-one signal?

    Answer: That the person is not your priority, which erodes trust

    Presence is the gift.

    04 · Empathy in Leadership¶

    Q13. What is the difference between cognitive and emotional empathy?

    Answer: Cognitive empathy understands what someone thinks and feels; emotional empathy feels with them

    Leaders need both, with boundaries.

    Q14. Does empathy mean agreeing with someone?

    Answer: No: you can understand their experience and still decline or decide differently

    Empathy is understanding, not agreement.

    Q15. What is a risk of too much emotional empathy without boundaries?

    Answer: Burnout and avoiding hard decisions to spare feelings

    Balance care with clarity.

    Q16. Which is an empathic question?

    Answer: 'What is this like for you?'

    Open and curious.

    05 · Building Trust¶

    Q17. The trust equation is trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) / self-orientation. What trust score results from these ratings (1-10)?

    c,r,i,s=8,7,6,3
print(round((c+r+i)/s,1))

    Answer: 7.0

    High self-orientation (focus on your own agenda) divides trust down: (8+7+6)/3 = 7.0. Verified by running it.

    Q18. With the same equation, what happens if credibility, reliability and intimacy are 8, 7, 6 but self-orientation rises from 3 to 7?

    print(round((8+7+6)/3,1), round((8+7+6)/7,1))

    Answer: 7.0 3.0

    Trust collapses when people sense you are mainly serving yourself. Verified by running it.

    Q19. Which is a small moment that builds trust?

    Answer: Doing what you said you would do, when you said you would

    Trust accumulates in small, consistent acts.

    Q20. What is the first step in repairing broken trust?

    Answer: Acknowledge the specific harm without excuses, then change behaviour

    Repair needs ownership and action.

    06 · Empowering Others (Delegation Basics)¶

    Q21. Which is a common reason leaders under-delegate?

    Answer: They believe it is faster to do it themselves and fear loss of quality or control

    The short-term cost hides the long-term cost.

    Q22. Delegation levels run from 1 (tell) to 7 (delegate fully). If a task is at level 4 and you move it up two levels, what level is it?

    lvl=4
lvl=min(7,lvl+2)
print(lvl)

    Answer: 6

    Move authority in steps as competence and trust grow. Verified by running it.

    Q23. What makes delegation different from dumping?

    Answer: Clear outcome, authority, resources and support with a check-in rhythm

    Delegate the outcome, not the steps.

    Q24. A new hire has low experience on a task. Which delegation level fits?

    Answer: A lower level with more guidance and checks, increasing as they grow

    Match delegation to competence and context.

    07 · Foundational Communication Skills¶

    Q25. What is the key principle of foundational communication?

    Answer: Clarity over cleverness: say what you mean in the simplest accurate words

    Reduce the chance of misunderstanding.

    Q26. What does the message-audience-medium check ask?

    Answer: Is this the right message for this audience in this channel?

    Sensitive news does not belong in a group chat.

    Q27. Which is the best way to frame a request?

    Answer: State the outcome, the reason and what you need, then ask for questions

    Clear requests get better results.

    Q28. What should you do after delivering an important message?

    Answer: Check understanding by asking the listener to say it back in their words

    Confirm shared understanding.

    08 · Feedback Basics (Giving and Receiving)¶

    Q29. What does the SBI feedback framework stand for?

    Answer: Situation, Behavior, Impact

    Describe when, what you saw and its effect.

    Q30. Which is an SBI statement?

    Answer: 'In Tuesday's client call (S), you interrupted twice (B), and the client stopped sharing details (I).'

    Specific and observable, not a judgement.

    Q31. Why should praise also be specific?

    Answer: It tells the person exactly what to repeat

    Vague praise does not teach.

    Q32. What is the best timing for corrective feedback?

    Answer: Soon after the event, in private, with time to talk

    Timely and private.

    09 · Serving vs. Directing (The Mindset Shift)¶

    Q33. What is the mindset shift in 'serving vs. directing'?

    Answer: Defaulting to asking what the person needs to succeed rather than telling them what to do

    Direct when needed, but start from service.

    Q34. Why is the directing default so sticky?

    Answer: It feels faster, rewards the leader's expertise and is what most organisations model

    Habits and incentives reinforce it.

    Q35. In a crisis with a clear safe procedure, what is appropriate?

    Answer: Directing clearly and quickly, then debriefing and developing the team afterwards

    Servant leaders choose the mode that serves.

    Q36. How can you notice you have slipped into directing?

    Answer: You are giving answers more than asking questions, and people stop bringing problems

    Watch the signals.

    10 · Project — Personal Servant Leadership Philosophy Statement¶

    Q37. What is the aim of the personal philosophy statement project?

    Answer: To articulate your values, commitments and how you will behave as a servant leader

    A living reference for decisions.

    Q38. Which element belongs in a philosophy statement?

    Answer: Concrete behaviours you will practise, not just values words

    Behaviours make values testable.

    Q39. How should you test your philosophy statement?

    Answer: Ask a trusted colleague whether your behaviour matches it

    Feedback closes the gap.

    Q40. How often should you revisit it?

    Answer: Regularly, such as quarterly or after major experiences

    It should evolve.

    Level 2 · Intermediate¶

    01 · Coaching Fundamentals for Leaders¶

    Q41. What does GROW stand for in coaching?

    Answer: Goal, Reality, Options, Will

    A simple conversation structure.

    Q42. Which is a coaching question in the Reality step?

    Answer: 'What is happening now, and what have you tried?'

    Understand before generating options.

    Q43. What is the difference between coaching and advising?

    Answer: Coaching helps the person find their own answers through questions; advising supplies the answer

    Use advice sparingly and deliberately.

    Q44. Which closing question belongs in the Will step?

    Answer: 'What will you do, by when, and what might get in the way?'

    Commit to action.

    02 · Conflict Resolution as a Servant Leader¶

    Q45. Which conflict mode is right when the issue is trivial and the relationship matters more?

    Answer: Accommodating or avoiding, briefly

    No mode is always best.

    Q46. What are the five Thomas-Kilmann conflict modes?

    Answer: Competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding and accommodating

    Each fits certain situations.

    Q47. What is the difference between positions and interests?

    Answer: A position is what someone says they want; an interest is why they want it

    Solve for interests to find options.

    Q48. Two teams each have one desk-booking slot (position: 'the room all day'). Their interests: A needs a quiet hour for calls; B needs the whiteboard for 90 minutes. How many minutes of overlap in a day room schedule are needed if A takes 60 and B takes 90 back to back?

    a,b=60,90
print(a+b)

    Answer: 150

    Looking at interests shows both needs fit in 150 minutes, no winner required. Verified by running it.

    03 · Building Psychological Safety¶

    Q49. What is psychological safety?

    Answer: A shared belief that you can speak up, ask or admit mistakes without being punished or humiliated

    Coined by Amy Edmondson.

    Q50. What is psychological safety NOT?

    Answer: Lowering standards or avoiding hard feedback

    High safety plus high standards drives performance.

    Q51. Which leader behaviour most builds psychological safety?

    Answer: Responding to bad news with curiosity and thanks

    People repeat what is rewarded.

    Q52. Which is a quick diagnostic?

    Answer: Ask the team anonymously if it is safe to take a risk and how mistakes are treated

    Use a short survey and discuss results.

    04 · Team Development Stages (Tuckman's Model)¶

    Q53. What are Tuckman's stages in order?

    Answer: Forming, storming, norming, performing (and later adjourning)

    Teams may regress when members change.

    Q54. Your new team is polite but unclear on roles and cautious. Which stage is this?

    Answer: Forming

    Provide clarity and direction.

    Q55. A team is arguing over how to work. Which stage and which response?

    Answer: Storming: facilitate conflict resolution and clarify norms

    Storming is normal and necessary.

    Q56. A high-performing team loses two members and a new lead arrives. What often happens?

    Answer: The team can drop back a stage and needs re-forming

    Stages are not strictly one-way.

    05 · Delegation Deep Dive¶

    Q57. Seven delegation levels run from 1 (tell) to 7 (delegate). If an employee is at level 5 (advise) and you want them to reach 7, how many steps remain?

    print(7-5)

    Answer: 2

    Move one level at a time and review. Verified by running it.

    Q58. Which belongs in a delegation contract?

    Answer: Outcome, authority, resources, deadline, check-ins and what 'good' looks like

    Clarity prevents rework.

    Q59. What is reverse delegation?

    Answer: The delegate hands the problem back to you; respond with a question

    'What do you recommend?'

    Q60. Why delegate meaningful work and not only chores?

    Answer: It develops people and signals trust

    Growth matters.

    06 · Difficult Conversations¶

    Q61. Why does the opening sentence decide a difficult conversation?

    Answer: It sets the frame: a blaming opening triggers defence; a curious, clear opening invites dialogue

    Start with facts and purpose.

    Q62. Which is a better opening?

    Answer: 'I'd like to talk about the missed deadlines, because I want us to find what is getting in the way.'

    Describe, not label.

    Q63. What should you prepare beforehand?

    Answer: Facts, your intent, the other's likely view and the outcome you want

    Preparation reduces emotion.

    Q64. What do you do if the other person becomes very emotional?

    Answer: Pause, acknowledge the feeling and continue when ready

    Emotion blocks listening.

    07 · Leading Through Change¶

    Q65. What does Bridges' model say about change and transition?

    Answer: Change is external; transition is the internal psychological process of letting go and adjusting

    Leaders must manage the transition.

    Q66. Which question are people really asking in change?

    Answer: 'What does this mean for me?'

    Address the personal impact.

    Q67. Which behaviour helps most through change?

    Answer: Communicate often and honestly, including what you do not know

    Uncertainty breeds rumours.

    Q68. Why involve people in designing the change?

    Answer: Ownership reduces resistance and improves solutions

    Participation matters.

    08 · Recognizing & Developing Talent¶

    Q69. What is the difference between performance and potential?

    Answer: Performance is current results; potential is capacity to grow into bigger roles

    Do not promote on performance alone.

    Q70. Which is an observable signal of potential?

    Answer: Learns quickly from feedback and seeks stretch work

    Look for learning agility.

    Q71. Why is it risky to rely on gut feel to spot talent?

    Answer: It encodes bias and favours people like you

    Use structured observation.

    Q72. How do you develop talent?

    Answer: Stretch assignments with support, feedback and sponsorship

    70-20-10 mix.

    09 · Servant Leadership in Meetings¶

    Q73. A one-hour meeting with 8 people at an average loaded cost of 60 per hour per person costs how much?

    print(8*60*1)

    Answer: 480

    Meetings are expensive; design each one for a clear job. Verified by running it.

    Q74. What is the 'one meeting, one job' idea?

    Answer: Each meeting should have a single purpose such as decide, inform or generate options

    Mixed purposes waste time.

    Q75. What does a servant leader do in meetings?

    Answer: Creates space for quieter voices and protects the agenda and outcomes

    Facilitate rather than dominate.

    Q76. Which is a good meeting design element?

    Answer: A clear outcome, an agenda with time and named owners for actions

    Close with decisions and owners.

    10 · Project — A Coaching Plan for a Direct Report¶

    Q77. What is the aim of the coaching plan project?

    Answer: To plan a series of coaching conversations for one direct report toward a goal

    Use GROW and track progress.

    Q78. What should the plan include?

    Answer: Goal, strengths, development areas, session rhythm, support and measures of progress

    Make it measurable.

    Q79. How do you know the coaching is working?

    Answer: The person takes more ownership and shows observable progress against their goal

    Look for behaviour change.

    Q80. What do you do if the report does not take actions?

    Answer: Explore barriers with curiosity, then adjust the plan or expectations

    Accountability with care.

    Level 3 · Advanced¶

    01 · Organizational Culture & Servant Leadership¶

    Q81. According to the course, culture is best defined by...

    Answer: What actually gets rewarded and tolerated, not what is written on the wall

    Behaviour is the evidence.

    Q82. What does a culture audit look at?

    Answer: Stories, rewards, decisions and who is promoted or excluded

    Compare stated and lived values.

    Q83. A company says 'we value teamwork' but promotes only lone stars. What is the real culture?

    Answer: Individual heroics

    Incentives speak louder.

    Q84. What is the leader's most powerful culture lever?

    Answer: Consistently modelling and rewarding the desired behaviours

    People copy what leaders do.

    02 · Leading Without Formal Authority¶

    Q85. Why does 'just escalate it' often fail?

    Answer: It adds delay and conflict and ignores the other party's interests

    Build influence first.

    Q86. What is a currency map?

    Answer: A list of what the other party values (recognition, resources, learning) that you can offer

    Influence runs on exchange of value.

    Q87. How do you frame a request to someone you do not manage?

    Answer: In terms of their goals and reasoning, not just your need

    Make it their win.

    Q88. What builds influence without authority?

    Answer: Credibility, relationships and helping others succeed first

    Give before you ask.

    03 · Systems Thinking for Leaders¶

    Q89. What is the 'tell' that you have a system problem?

    Answer: The same problem recurs with different people

    Look at structures, not blame.

    Q90. What does a reinforcing loop do?

    Answer: Amplifies change in the same direction (growth or collapse)

    Look for balancing loops too.

    Q91. Late deliveries lead to pressure, which causes shortcuts, which causes defects, which causes rework and more lateness. What is this?

    Answer: A reinforcing (vicious) loop

    Break the loop at a leverage point.

    Q92. Which is a high-leverage intervention?

    Answer: Changing the structure or rule that drives the behaviour

    Fix causes, not symptoms.

    04 · Advanced Coaching Techniques (the GROW Model)¶

    Q93. Why does the order of GROW matter?

    Answer: Jumping to options before clarifying goal and reality leads to weak solutions

    Slow down to go fast.

    Q94. What is a stretching Goal question?

    Answer: 'What would success look like if this went really well?'

    Inspire and clarify.

    Q95. When should you offer advice in coaching?

    Answer: After asking, and with permission, as one option among others

    Keep ownership with the coachee.

    Q96. What makes a Will step strong?

    Answer: A specific action, date and a plan for obstacles

    Commitment and follow-up.

    05 · Building High-Performing Teams¶

    Q97. What are conditions of high-performing teams?

    Answer: Clear purpose, trust and safety, roles, healthy conflict and accountability

    Order of leverage: trust first.

    Q98. A team has stars but poor trust. What is the likely result?

    Answer: Hidden conflict, poor collaboration and underperformance

    Skills without trust stall.

    Q99. How do you run a team diagnostic?

    Answer: Ask structured questions on the conditions and discuss the gaps openly

    Share and act on results.

    Q100. Which team behaviour signals accountability?

    Answer: Peers hold each other to commitments, not just the leader

    Mutual accountability.

    06 · Leading Remote/Distributed Teams with Servant Leadership¶

    Q101. What breaks first in a distributed team?

    Answer: Informal trust and context, because spontaneous contact disappears

    Compensate deliberately.

    Q102. Which practice helps remote trust?

    Answer: Clear written norms, regular one-to-ones and shared visibility of work

    Output over presence.

    Q103. Why is monitoring keystrokes harmful?

    Answer: It signals distrust and drives gaming rather than results

    Trust and outcomes.

    Q104. How should you handle time zones?

    Answer: Rotate inconvenient meeting times and use asynchronous documentation

    Fairness builds trust.

    07 · Ethical Leadership & Decision-Making¶

    Q105. What makes a decision an ethical dilemma?

    Answer: Two or more legitimate values or duties conflict, so any choice costs something

    Right versus right.

    Q106. What is a sound ethical decision step?

    Answer: Identify stakeholders, test options against principles, consult and choose transparently

    Be able to explain it publicly.

    Q107. What is a simple test for an ethical choice?

    Answer: Would I be comfortable if this appeared on the front page and had to explain it to those affected?

    Transparency check.

    Q108. What should a leader do when a policy conflicts with doing right?

    Answer: Raise it, seek change and be transparent about the trade-offs

    Courage and process.

    08 · Mentorship Programs¶

    Q109. Why do most mentorship programs fail by month two?

    Answer: No structure, unclear expectations and no one owns follow-through

    Design for the awkward middle.

    Q110. What does a good mentorship design include?

    Answer: Clear purpose, matching, meeting rhythm, guidelines and a check-in with participants

    Support and measure.

    Q111. What makes a good mentor?

    Answer: Listens, shares experience without prescribing and supports the mentee's goals

    Ask more than tell.

    Q112. How do you evaluate a program?

    Answer: Participation, goals progress and mentee and mentor feedback

    Measure outcomes.

    09 · Measuring Leadership Effectiveness¶

    Q113. Why is gut feel a poor instrument for leadership effectiveness?

    Answer: It is biased and untracked; use a set of signals over time

    Collect evidence.

    Q114. Which belongs in a leadership signal set?

    Answer: Team engagement, retention, development of others and delivery results

    Balanced indicators.

    Q115. What is a lagging indicator?

    Answer: An outcome after the fact, such as attrition

    Pair with leading indicators.

    Q116. What do you do with the measures?

    Answer: Discuss them with the team and act on what they show

    Close the loop.

    10 · Project — A Team Development Plan¶

    Q117. What is the aim of the team development plan?

    Answer: To move a team toward higher performance with stage-appropriate actions and measures

    Diagnose, plan, act, review.

    Q118. Which element belongs in the plan?

    Answer: Diagnosis, goals, actions with owners, support and review dates

    Make it trackable.

    Q119. Who should co-create the plan?

    Answer: The team itself, so it owns the changes

    Involvement builds commitment.

    Q120. How often should you review progress?

    Answer: Regularly, with a defined cadence such as monthly

    Adjust quickly.

    Level 4 · Master¶

    01 · Servant Leadership at the Executive Level¶

    Q121. What doesn't scale as you grow into an executive?

    Answer: Your personal presence and direct involvement in every decision

    Scale through leaders and systems.

    Q122. What is the leader-of-leaders shift?

    Answer: Moving from doing and deciding to developing leaders who decide well

    Your output is others' leadership.

    Q123. What should executives protect?

    Answer: Time to listen to the front line and keep context

    Avoid information filters.

    Q124. How can executives stay grounded?

    Answer: Regularly talk to customers and front-line staff

    Direct contact.

    02 · Building a Servant Leadership Culture Org-Wide¶

    Q125. Why does culture-by-example not scale beyond one team?

    Answer: Not everyone sees the leader; systems like hiring, promotion and rewards must carry it

    Embed in systems.

    Q126. What are systems for embedding servant leadership?

    Answer: Selection and promotion, development and recognition, and performance measures

    Three-system framework.

    A team-health survey analyser¶

    Analyse a small, made-up team survey: rank items by average, flag items below a threshold, compute a psychological-safety index from the relevant items and detect polarised answers. # A team-health survey analyser (illustrative data, not real research data)
SURVEY = {
    "I can admit a mistake without fear": [4, 5, 3, 4, 2, 4],
    "Bad news is received with curiosity": [3, 3, 2, 4, 2, 3],
    "I know what is expected of me": [5, 4, 5, 5, 4, 5],
    "My manager helps me grow": [4, 4, 5, 3, 4, 4],
    "Decisions are explained": [2, 3, 2, 3, 2, 2],
}
SAFETY_ITEMS = ["I can admit a mistake without fear", "Bad news is received with curiosity"]

def mean(xs):
    return sum(xs) / len(xs)

def summarise(survey, threshold=3.5):
    rows = []
    for item, scores in survey.items():
        rows.append((item, round(mean(scores), 2), min(scores), "ACT" if mean(scores) < threshold else "ok"))
    return sorted(rows, key=lambda r: (r[1], r[0]))

def safety_index(survey, items):
    return round(mean([mean(survey[i]) for i in items]), 2)

def spread_flag(scores):
    return max(scores) - min(scores) >= 3

for item, avg, low, flag in summarise(SURVEY):
    print(f"{avg:4.2f} {flag:3} low={low} {item}")
print("safety index:", safety_index(SURVEY, SAFETY_ITEMS))
print("polarised:", [i for i, s in SURVEY.items() if spread_flag(s)])
```

Averages hide disagreement, so the spread check surfaces an item where some people feel safe and others do not. The sort puts the weakest items first so the discussion starts where it matters. Verified by running the solution.

    A delegation planner and meeting cost¶

    Model the seven delegation levels: move a person up when competence and kept commitments are strong, down after repeated missed commitments, and compute what a meeting really costs. This is a simple, illustrative rule set to practise reasoning, not a management algorithm; real decisions need conversation and context.

# A delegation planner (a simple model, not a validated assessment)
LEVELS = {1: "tell", 2: "sell", 3: "consult", 4: "agree", 5: "advise", 6: "inquire", 7: "delegate"}

def next_level(level, competence, trust_events, missed_commitments):
    """competence 1-5; trust_events = kept promises this period; missed_commitments = broken ones"""
    if missed_commitments >= 2:
        return max(1, level - 1)
    if competence >= 4 and trust_events >= 3 and missed_commitments == 0:
        return min(7, level + 1)
    return level

def meeting_cost(people, minutes, hourly):
    return round(people * hourly * minutes / 60, 2)

team = [
    ("Ana", 3, 4, 4, 0),
    ("Bo", 5, 2, 3, 2),
    ("Cy", 6, 5, 5, 0),
    ("Di", 7, 5, 6, 0),
]
for name, level, comp, kept, missed in team:
    new = next_level(level, comp, kept, missed)
    arrow = "up" if new > level else "down" if new < level else "same"
    print(f"{name}: {LEVELS[level]} -> {LEVELS[new]} ({arrow})")
print("weekly sync cost:", meeting_cost(8, 45, 60))
print("fewer, better:", meeting_cost(5, 30, 60))
```

Encoding the rules makes the thresholds explicit and debatable, which is the point: delegation should change in small steps tied to evidence. The meeting cost shows why each hour needs a purpose. Verified by running the solution.
