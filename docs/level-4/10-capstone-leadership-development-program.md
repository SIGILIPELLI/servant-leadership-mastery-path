# 10 · Capstone — Design an Org-Wide Leadership Development Program

Every project in this track has scaled up: a philosophy statement in
Level 1, a coaching plan for one person in Level 2, a team development
plan in Level 3. This capstone pulls the entire track together into its
largest form — a real, implementable leadership development program for
an organization, built on the same evidence-based discipline as every
plan before it. The test of completion is not an elegant document. It is
whether a real organization could hand this to HR and a budget owner and
actually run it, and whether it would produce leaders whose teams are
measurably better off, not just leaders who attended more training.

## 1. The brief

Design a full leadership development program for a real or realistic
organization (your own, if you have one; a plausible one otherwise).
Produce a written program with the eight sections below, drawing
explicitly on the frameworks from across all four levels — this capstone
should read as the synthesis of the entire track, not a fresh invention.

| Program section | Draws on |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic: the actual gap this program closes | L3 Mod 9, L4 Mod 2 |
| Target population and selection | L4 Mod 3 (succession), L4 Mod 5 (equity) |
| Curriculum spine | This track's own four-level structure |
| The embedding mechanism (survives beyond the program) | L4 Mod 2 |
| Sponsorship and mentorship structure | L3 Mod 8 |
| Measurement plan, with a control group or baseline | L3 Mod 9 |
| Budget and resourcing reality | — |
| Failure conditions, decided in advance | L4 Mod 4 |

Three hard rules, escalated from the earlier projects' discipline.
**Diagnose before designing** — the program must be built from a real or
realistic gap-finding exercise, not from a generic list of leadership
competencies. **Name what would make you kill the program** — a program
with no stated failure condition is not a program, it's a permanent
fixture nobody will ever have the evidence to end or fix. And **show the
equity check** — who this program will and won't reach by default, and
what you did about that gap specifically (Level 4, Module 5).

## 3. Worked example (condensed — a full submission runs longer)

**Organization.** Halden Group, a 450-person logistics company. Three
consecutive engineering director hires from outside have failed within
18 months; internal promotion to director has a 90% first-year retention
rate by contrast. The diagnosis points at a specific gap: strong senior
individual contributors are promoted to director with no structured
transition support, while external director hires are onboarded with a
formal 90-day plan the internal promotions never receive.

```text
1. DIAGNOSTIC
   Gap: internal promotions to director have no structured
     transition, while external hires do — the reverse of what
     the retention data would suggest is needed.
   Evidence: exit interviews of 4 of 6 internal directors who
     left within 2 years cite "wasn't clear what changed about
     the job," a systems-level (L3 Mod 3) signal, not a
     competence one.
   Why a program: this is a repeated pattern across multiple
     people in the same role, not one individual's struggle —
     the L3 Mod 3 test for a structural cause, not a people one.

2. TARGET POPULATION
   Senior ICs identified via the Succession Matrix (L4 Mod 3)
     as "ready in 1-2 years" for a director role.
   Equity check: the current informal identification method
     surfaces mostly people already visible to VPs socially.
     Fix: every senior IC with 3+ years tenure gets a structured
     nomination review, not just VP-initiated nominations.

3. CURRICULUM SPINE
   L1 equivalent: foundational listening/feedback refresher —
     self-paced, 2 weeks, for ICs who've never formally managed.
   L2 equivalent: coaching and delegation — cohort-based, with
     a real coaching plan (L2 capstone) built for an actual
     direct report within the first 90 days in role.
   L3 equivalent: team diagnostic and culture audit, run on
     their own new team in months 3-6, with a mentor reviewing.
   L4 equivalent: only for those who progress toward VP —
     succession and systems thinking, cohort, year two.
   Format: months 1-2 self-paced + cohort sessions, months 3-6
     applied with a named mentor, ongoing mentor relationship
     through year one.

4. EMBEDDING MECHANISM
   Promotion rubric (L4 Mod 2) gets one added, scored line:
     "has a named 90-day transition plan and mentor" — required
     for the promotion to be finalized, not optional afterward.

5. SPONSORSHIP STRUCTURE
   Each new director matched with a director two years senior,
     not their own manager — six structured sessions in the
     first 90 days, explicit exit ramp if the match isn't
     working (L3 Mod 8) at the 6-week check.

6. MEASUREMENT PLAN
   Baseline: current 18-month retention rate for internally
     promoted directors (currently ~70%, below the 90%
     first-year figure once the second year is included).
   Signal: 18-month retention of program cohort vs. the prior
     three years' promoted cohort, plus exit interview theme
     tracking (L3 Mod 9).
   Re-measured: 18 months after each cohort's promotion date.

7. BUDGET
   Facilitator time: ~40 hours/cohort. Mentor time: ~12 hours
     per pairing over 90 days, unpaid but counted against
     mentor's own goals. Deprioritized to fund: one external
     leadership-training vendor contract not renewed.

8. FAILURE CONDITIONS
   If 18-month retention for the first two cohorts doesn't
     improve over the 70% baseline by at least 10 points, the
     program's curriculum gets redesigned, not just extended
     for more cohorts on faith.
```

## 4. Rubric — mark your own program

| Criterion | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic | Generic "we need better leaders" | A specific, evidenced, structural gap |
| Equity check | Absent or symbolic | Names who's excluded by default, with a specific fix |
| Embedding | A program that ends when the budget does | A rubric or system change that survives it |
| Measurement | Satisfaction surveys only | A hard-to-fake signal against a real baseline |
| Failure condition | Absent | A specific number, by a specific date, decided before launch |

## Exercise

Build the full program using the template in section 2 for a real or
realistic organization, running the diagnostic honestly before you
design anything else. Pay particular attention to section 8 — write the
actual number and date that would make you kill or redesign this
program, not a vague aspiration, before you present it to anyone who
might fund it.

## Stretch goals

- **Pilot one cohort with real people**, even informally, before
  proposing the full program — the gap between a designed curriculum and
  what a real cohort actually needs is usually large.
- **Have someone from the target population review the diagnostic and
  equity check** before you finalize them — the people the program is
  meant to serve will catch gaps a designer working alone will miss.
- **Design the version of this program that survives your own
  departure** (Level 4, Module 8) — name who owns it if you leave next
  year, not just who runs it while you're there.
- **Present the failure conditions to whoever funds the program before
  launch**, out loud, and get their agreement in advance that hitting
  them means real change — a failure condition nobody agreed to in
  advance is not one that will actually be honored under pressure.
- **Revisit your own Level 1 philosophy statement** from the very start
  of this track, and write one paragraph on what changed in how you'd
  state it now, having built everything from an individual relationship
  up to an organization-wide program. That comparison is the real
  measure of what this track was for.
