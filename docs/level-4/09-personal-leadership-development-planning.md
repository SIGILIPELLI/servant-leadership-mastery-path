# 09 · Personal Leadership Development Planning

This entire track has asked you to build development plans for other
people — a report in Level 2, a team in Level 3. This module turns the
same rigor on yourself, at the point in a career where formal
development programs and an assigned manager's coaching often thin out
the most, precisely when the stakes of getting your own growth wrong are
highest. A senior leader's development stalls not from lack of ambition
but from lack of the same structure they'd insist on for anyone else —
this module supplies that structure, applied to you.

## 1. Why senior leaders' own development quietly stalls

Three specific mechanisms: less honest feedback reaches you, because
status makes disagreement costly for others (Level 3, Module 9); there
is often no one whose formal job is coaching you the way you coach your
own reports; and success itself removes the forcing function — a leader
who is clearly succeeding by visible metrics has no obvious signal
telling them which specific behavior needs to change next, even when
something does.

## 2. The Personal Leadership Development Plan

Deliberately built on the same template as Level 2's coaching plan
project — the discipline of building your own plan the way you'd build
one for someone else is itself the point.

```text
PERSONAL LEADERSHIP DEVELOPMENT PLAN
Period: ______ to ______   Reviewed with: ______________

1. GOAL (in your own words — what do you actually want to be
   true of your leadership in 12-18 months?)
   ______________________________________________

2. REALITY (gathered from hard-to-fake signals, Level 3 Mod 9 —
   not your own impression)
   What the signals actually say, with sources named:
   What you've been avoiding looking at directly:
   ______________________________________________

3. THE ONE GAP (a behavior, not a trait — same discipline as
   coaching someone else)
   ______________________________________________

4. THE STRETCH — real, needed work that forces the gap closed
   What it is, and why it can't be avoided by delegating around it:
   ______________________________________________

5. THE CONVERSATION YOU'RE AVOIDING
   Who, about what, and your prepared opening sentence:
   ______________________________________________

6. WHO WILL TELL YOU THE TRUTH (name specific people, not "my
   network" — a personal board of people who owe you nothing
   and will say the hard thing)
   ______________________________________________

7. CHECKPOINTS
   Dates, and the specific hard-to-fake signal you'll check
   at each one:
   ______________________________________________
```

## 3. Worked example

**Subject.** Kavita, COO of a 300-person company, eleven years into
leadership roles. External signals are strong; she's respected and the
business performs well. A recent 360, run at her own request rather than
required by the company, surfaces something she hadn't been looking for.

```text
PERSONAL LEADERSHIP DEVELOPMENT PLAN
Period: 1 October to 31 March   Reviewed with: my mentor, Bashir

1. GOAL
   "I want to stop being the reason decisions bottleneck at the
    exec level, and actually know that because I've tested it,
    not because I believe it about myself."

2. REALITY
   Signals: 360 shows my directors rate me high on strategic
     judgment and noticeably lower ("needs improvement," 4 of 6
     respondents) on "makes decisions without me needing to be
     in the room." Regrettable attrition is low, which had made
     me assume this wasn't an issue.
   What I'd been avoiding: I schedule myself into meetings I'm
     not needed for because I tell myself I "add value," and I've
     never actually tested what happens when I'm not there.

3. THE ONE GAP
   Behavior: I insert myself into decisions that are already
     delegated, because staying informed feels like diligence
     rather than what it actually is for my directors — a
     signal that they still need my sign-off.

4. THE STRETCH
   What: block two full weeks with zero standing meetings with
     my direct reports' teams — genuinely unreachable except for
     a real emergency, not a soft "available if needed."
   Why it can't be avoided: the only way to find out whether
     decisions actually happen without me is to remove myself
     completely, not partially.

5. THE CONVERSATION I'M AVOIDING
   Who/what: my CFO, Tomas, who I suspect has been quietly
     routing decisions through me that are actually his to make,
     because I've never told him not to.
   Opening sentence: "I think I've been making it easy for you to
     bring me decisions that are already yours, and I want to
     change that starting now, not because of anything you've
     done wrong."

6. WHO WILL TELL ME THE TRUTH
   Bashir (external mentor, no reporting relationship, will say
     the unflattering thing). My EA, Priya, who sees my calendar
     patterns better than I do and has permission to flag when
     I've booked myself into something I said I'd stay out of.

7. CHECKPOINTS
   1 Nov: did the two-week block actually happen with zero
     exceptions, checked against my actual calendar, not my
     intention.
   1 Jan: 360 re-run on the single item — "makes decisions
     without me in the room."
   Signal it's not working: if by January the score hasn't moved
     and I find a reasonable-sounding excuse for why the two
     weeks weren't a fair test.
```

## 4. The role of a personal board of truth-tellers

Section 6 of the template is the piece most senior leaders skip, and the
one that matters most as formal feedback mechanisms thin out with
seniority. The criteria for who belongs on this list: they have nothing
to gain by flattering you, they've demonstrated willingness to say an
unwelcome thing to you before, and you genuinely act on what they tell
you rather than just hearing it. A list of three real names who meet all
three criteria is worth more than any annual 360 process.

## 5. Testing yourself the way you'd test anyone else

The discipline that makes this plan different from a New Year's
resolution is the same one from Level 2's coaching plan: the stretch
assignment has to be real, unavoidable work, not a symbolic gesture.
Kavita's two-week block is a genuine test with a genuine risk (something
could actually go wrong in her absence) — a development plan with no
real risk in it is not testing anything.

## 6. Reviewing the plan with someone else, not just yourself

A personal development plan reviewed only in your own head has the same
weakness as a coaching plan you write for someone else without ever
showing them — it stays comfortable because nobody is checking it
against reality. Review yours with at least one person from section 6 on
a real calendar cadence, and let them ask you the Level 2 GROW questions
about your own goal rather than only asking yourself.

## Exercise

Build your own Personal Leadership Development Plan using the template
in section 2, gathering the Reality section from actual hard-to-fake
signals (Level 3, Module 9) rather than self-assessment. Name your real
personal board of truth-tellers in section 6 — three specific people,
not a category — and schedule the first review of this plan with one of
them within the next month, the way Kavita did with Bashir.
